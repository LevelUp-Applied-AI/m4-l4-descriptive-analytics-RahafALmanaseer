# Findings

## Observations
- GPA mostly between 2.5 and 3.5, with a few students below 2.5
- Students who study more hours per week tend to achieve higher GPA
- Internship students generally perform better than those without internships
- Attendance varies slightly; lower attendance correlates with lower GPA
- Study habits differ by department, but GPA distribution is fairly similar across departments

## Tests
- T-test (internship vs GPA):
  - t = 13.56
  - p ≈ 0.00
  - Interpretation: Students with internships have significantly higher GPA. This result is statistically significant.
- ANOVA (GPA by department):
  - F = 0.67
  - p = 0.61
  - Interpretation: There is no statistically significant difference in GPA across departments. The effect of department on GPA is minimal.

## Insights
- Encouraging students to take internships could improve overall academic performance
- Promoting regular study habits is important for better GPA outcomes
- Monitoring attendance could help identify students at risk and provide targeted support
- Differences between departments are small; focus on student-level support rather than department-level interventions
